## Hypothesis & Architecture

Legacy government exam portals in India are cluttered with aggressive ads and broken links. I hypothesized that building a clean, automated portal (`onestopread.com`) using:
1. Python scrapers for official government notification PDFs,
2. Gemini Flash for structured date/fee/eligibility summarization,
3. Programmatic Next.js static pages with structured schema,

would rapidly capture search engine share for long-tail notification queries.

## What Actually Happened

- **The Early Spike (Days 1–20):** Search engine bots crawled the clean sitemaps quickly. Daily clicks climbed from 2 to 30 per day, and daily impressions rose past 800.
- **The Algorithmic Cliff (Days 21–28):** Around day 25, search engines updated their indexing signals. Daily impressions flatlined to under 20, and clicks dropped to 0.
- **Attempted Recovery:** Replaced automated copy with hand-edited summaries, added direct primary source links to official government websites, and audited metadata. The domain remained in algorithmic penalty status.

## Key Learnings

1. Search algorithms flag repetitive syntax patterns and high publication volume on zero-authority domains.
2. Building standalone utility tools (like [PhotoResizer](/projects/photoresizer)) generates compounding organic traffic because interactive tools provide genuine utility that generic content aggregators cannot replicate.
