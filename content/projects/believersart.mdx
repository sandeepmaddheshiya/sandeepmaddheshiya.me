## What is it?

BelieversArt is a custom full-stack e-commerce platform and admin management system built for an art client in Canada. The platform allows art collectors to browse sketch collections, view high-resolution artwork previews, purchase pieces via Stripe, and track orders through a dedicated customer dashboard.

## Why did I build it?

The client needed an independent, customized online storefront for their art brand rather than paying ongoing SaaS subscription fees on generic e-commerce builders. Key requirements included:

1. A clean, focused gallery layout with high-resolution art previews.
2. Complete autonomy over catalog management, art sizing variants, and pricing without recurring platform fees.
3. Automated payment workflows and instant transactional order emails.

## The problem

Standard off-the-shelf e-commerce builders imposed recurring monthly fees, transaction commissions, and limited customization for unique sketch variants (dimensions, paper weight, custom framing options), along with poor performance on high-resolution image portfolios.

## Building it

### 1. Storefront & App Architecture
- Built with **Next.js (App Router)** utilizing Server Components for fast page loads and clean indexing of product catalog pages.
- Client-side state managed the shopping cart, customer checkout inputs, and live currency calculations.

### 2. Headless Content Management
- Integrated **Strapi CMS** as the headless backend, providing the artist with a dedicated admin dashboard to publish new sketches, manage inventory stock, and update pricing without modifying code.

### 3. Payment Processing
- Integrated **Stripe Checkout** with webhook endpoints to handle asynchronous payment confirmations, verify transaction signatures, and update order statuses.

### 4. Transactional Messaging
- Integrated **Resend** for sending immediate order confirmation receipts to buyers and dispatch notifications to the seller.

## What happened

Delivered across a 2-month development cycle. The store handled real user purchases smoothly with verified Stripe payouts, and the client completed the 500 CAD contract payment.

## What I learned

1. **Webhook Idempotency:** Payment webhooks can be delivered more than once by gateways; designing order creation handlers with strict idempotency keys avoids duplicate order records.
2. **Headless CMS Workflows:** Decoupling the admin interface (Strapi) from the public storefront (Next.js) gave the client total editorial freedom while keeping the frontend performant.
