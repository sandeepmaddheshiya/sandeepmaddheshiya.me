## The Part-Time Dilemma

In early 2026, I was working as a full-time software developer at Collegedunia while developing side projects in the evenings and on weekends.

While juggling full-time enterprise responsibilities:
- I launched `photoresizer.in` and made my first online dollar on `photoresizer.us`.
- I completed 4–6 months of manual visa guideline research and started building [VisaReadyNow](/projects/visareadynow).
- I built and launched [BelieversArt](/projects/believersart), an e-commerce platform for a Canadian client.

However, as workload at Collegedunia increased, side-project velocity began to stall. Critical customer support emails went unanswered during work hours, bug fixes were delayed, and search rankings fluctuated when new features couldn't be shipped on schedule.

## The Turning Point

In 2024, my earlier mobile app [Resourcify](/failed-projects/resourcify) was suspended by Google Play after 3 unaddressed policy warnings because I lacked the bandwidth to push target SDK updates while managing full-time job commitments. 

I realized that building sustainable independent software products requires undivided focus and fast feedback loops.

On **July 31, 2026**, I formally left my software developer role at Collegedunia to dedicate 100% of my time to building independent micro-SaaS utilities.

## The First 60 Days

Going full-time immediately accelerated project outcomes:
1. Rebuilt the core conversion flows and automated processing pipelines on [VisaReadyNow](/projects/visareadynow), generating **$250+ USD** in direct customer sales.
2. Formatted and open-sourced our entire 190+ country specifications dataset on npm and PyPI on August 31, 2026.
3. Expanded [PhotoResizer](/projects/photoresizer) programmatic clusters, resulting in a September 2026 breakout to **1,000+ daily clicks** and **25,000+ daily impressions**.

Building full-time does not guarantee success, but it removes the friction between identifying a customer problem and deploying the fix to production.
